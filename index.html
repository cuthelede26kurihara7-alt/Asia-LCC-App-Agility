Measuring Business Agility Through Digital Behavior

How Airlines Adapt Their Digital Platforms in Asia’s Competitive Aviation Market

By Nana Kurihara | Data Journalism Project | August 2026

Why This Matters

The airline industry has always been shaped by competition.

For decades, airlines competed through aircraft fleets, international routes, and pricing strategies.

Today, competition is increasingly happening through digital experiences.

Mobile apps have become more than booking tools. They connect customers with travel services, loyalty programs, payments, and daily interactions with airlines.

But one question remains:

How can we measure whether a company is truly agile in a digital economy?

Research Question

Can digital behavior reveal business agility?

Business agility is often described as the ability to respond quickly to change. However, measuring agility is difficult.

This project uses airline mobile app updates as a visible indicator of how companies continuously adapt their digital platforms.

The analysis focuses on two questions:

  • How frequently do airlines improve their digital platforms?
  • What types of improvements do they prioritize?

The Data

I analyzed mobile app release notes from four airlines connected to the Asian aviation market:

  • AirAsia
  • Scoot
  • ZIPAIR
  • Japan Airlines (JAL)

Each release note was manually classified into four categories:

  • Customer Experience
  • Feature Development
  • Maintenance
  • Network Expansion

Data Source

Release notes were collected from Apple App Store version histories.

Measuring the Speed of Digital Adaptation

The first chart compares app update frequency between 2024 and 2026.

Update frequency provides one visible indicator of how actively airlines continue improving their digital platforms.

Different Airlines, Different Paths to Agility

The second visualization examines what airlines prioritize when improving their digital platforms.

What the Data Reveals

AirAsia: Scaling Customer Experience

AirAsia’s updates are strongly focused on customer experience.

As one of Southeast Asia’s largest low-cost carriers, AirAsia uses digital platforms to efficiently serve a large and diverse customer base.

Scoot: Continuous Digital Experimentation

Scoot shows a more balanced approach across customer experience, features, and maintenance.

This suggests a strategy focused on improving multiple digital touchpoints simultaneously.

ZIPAIR: Digital Infrastructure for Growth

ZIPAIR combines operational improvements with network expansion.

As a newer airline brand, its digital strategy reflects the need to build scalable systems while supporting growth.

JAL: Reliability as Digital Agility

JAL demonstrates a different form of agility.

Rather than focusing primarily on rapid feature changes, JAL emphasizes reliability and operational stability.

Conclusion

Digital agility does not have one definition.

The data shows that airlines adapt according to their business models.

Low-cost carriers often use digital platforms to improve scalability and customer interaction.

Full-service carriers focus more on reliability and trust.

Digital behavior can reveal how companies adapt.

By observing what companies improve, how frequently they improve, and where they invest their effort, we can better understand business agility in competitive markets.
